# Backup demo recording (T103)

A last-resort video of one full two-minute run (whitepaper §12), for when everything fails on the day.
**The video is not stored in git.** It lives on two laptops and a USB stick, and this page says where.

Recording the demo screens is our own deliberate recording, separate from the game itself (which records
nothing; see `docs/privacy.md`). Only record teammates, or people who have said yes.

## What to record (all at the same time)

| Source | How | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Eye screen** (`/eye/`, robot's-eye view) | screen recorder on the laptop showing it | 1080p, 30 fps, **system audio on** (narration and effects come from the sound page on this laptop) |
| **B. The table** | a phone on a tripod or held steady, showing the table, the robot and the person driving | 1080p, 30 fps, its own mic (records the music too) |

Screen recorders: **OBS Studio** (free, Windows/macOS/Linux, recommended: one scene per screen); or Windows
**Snipping Tool → Record** / **Win + Alt + R** (Xbox Game Bar); macOS **Cmd + Shift + 5**.

## Step by step

**Before (15 min):**
1. Install OBS on the eye laptop. Add a *Display Capture* (or *Window Capture* of the eye browser window) and
   *Desktop Audio*. Settings → Output → Recording format **mp4** (or mkv + remux), quality "High".
2. One OBS scene with the eye view full screen plus desktop audio is all you need; then you only sync the
   phone video.
3. Everything at full brightness, notifications off (Windows Focus / macOS Do Not Disturb), status page closed.
4. Charge the table phone, clear 2 GB of space, tripod placed so the whole table and the driver are in frame.
5. Run the demo once as a rehearsal.

**Recording (5 min):**
6. Start the screen recording(s), then the phone video.
7. **Sync clap:** stand in front of the table phone and clap once, loudly, while someone presses the gamepad
   **horn** button at the same time. The horn sound and the clap give one shared moment in every recording.
8. Run the full §12 script (two minutes): drive, show the castle, play a song (world change and narration),
   reach down in front of the robot (giant), set the teddy bear down and drive at it (threat and recoil), song
   change. Say the lines out loud.
9. Stop the phone, then the screen recording(s). Record 2–3 takes and keep the best.

**Editing (30–60 min):** free editors: **DaVinci Resolve**, **Shotcut**, or **Clipchamp** (built into Windows).
10. Import all clips. Line them up on the sync clap/horn (the spike in the audio waveform).
11. Layout: eye view full screen, cutting to the table phone video (or a corner picture-in-picture of it) for
    the "reach down in front of the robot" and "set the teddy bear down" moments, so viewers see both sides.
12. Keep the eye laptop's audio (narration and effects) as the main track. Mix in the phone's audio quietly
    for the room and music.
13. Add a title card ("Genrator: every genre generates a world") and, if wanted, captions for the spoken lines.
14. Export **H.264 MP4, 1080p, 30 fps**, ~10–20 Mbit/s. File name: `genrator-demo-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.mp4`.

**Store (5 min):**
15. Copy the file to **two laptops** (not a cloud-only folder; it must play with wifi off) and a USB stick.
16. **Test offline:** turn wifi off on each laptop, double-click the file, and play it start to end with sound.
17. Fill in the table below.

## On the day: when to use it

If after **2 minutes** of trying, the robot, the phone or the network still won't work: say *"Let us show
you a run we recorded earlier"*, play the video full screen with sound, and talk over it with the §12 lines.
Keep the robot on the table so judges can still pick it up.
